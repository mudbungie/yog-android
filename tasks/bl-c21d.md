Measured live 2026-09-29 against engine 0.0.74 (standing acceptor, yog bl-5276), four dials on a build with bl-97ed + bl-2ba5. Rung 3 (re-punch at cached endpoints from the port the call named) was never exercised in the foreground: (1) after HOME the held stream dies ~5 s later (bl-f677) and the ladder immediately re-punches in the BACKGROUND, where the phone logs 'the entry's name did not resolve' / 'no bootstrap node resolved' and no SYN reaches the engine; that spends the re-punch arm (bl-97ed's guard) and backs off, so the next foreground dial goes straight to a fresh call. (2) A concurrent ask while the held stream is lent out climbs 'beside' the line from a fresh Punch::bind(0) port and, once its call is written, that port becomes the entry's — in dial A port 44041 (whose call landed) was replaced by 39861, so no later re-punch could ride the NAT mapping the engine opened. Engine /doctor accepted stayed 0 across the session.

Deliver: (a) no climb while the app is not in the foreground — a backgrounded ladder parks (drops nothing it holds, starts nothing), and the first foreground ask after the return climbs; the app already knows its lifecycle (find the resume/pause hook the shell exposes to Rust); if bl-f677 later rules for a foreground service, this becomes the service's policy, so keep it one predicate. (b) A beside climb never replaces the entry's port: the entry's port is the port whose call LANDED (or the first bound, until one lands); a beside call names its own port for its own window and is forgotten. (c) On the first foreground ask after a background gap, rung 3 fires from the entry's port before any call (bl-97ed's 10 s preface bound stays the guard). Tests on the fake bench with a lifecycle toggle: backgrounded → no climb, no line dropped by the ladder itself; foreground return → re-punch from the landed port, served by a fake engine that accepts only that port; a beside call does not change the entry's port. Then re-verify live: /doctor accepted must go 0 → 1 on the return dial.
