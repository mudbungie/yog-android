Found under bl-3d62 while moving the level-M fit test to the roving mint's size (yog bl-9043 grows a mint to about 1729 bytes). src/symbol/tests.rs round-trips a payload through encode() at level M and back through crate::scan::decode (rxing). Probed payloads of 1567, 1650, 1700, 1729, 1800 and 2300 bytes, two payload shapes (a repeated 'x' and an a..z cycle) at module pitches 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8: the a..z payload at 1650 and 1700 bytes (a 153-module symbol) never reads back at any pitch, the repeated-x payload at 1729 and 1800 fails at pitch 4 only, and 2300 fails at pitches 5-8. Encode always succeeds, so the level-M fit claim holds; what is unproven is that THIS app's own reader reads every symbol this app's own encoder draws at these sizes, which is exactly the path a phone scanning another phone's mint takes. Open question: a defect in rxing's detector on synthetic, perfectly aligned images (a camera frame is never that) or in the reader proper. First step: the same probe through a second decoder, or through rxing with TRY_HARDER / pure-barcode hints, to tell the two apart. bl-3d62's test pins the a..z payload at 1729, which reads back, and says nothing about the others.
