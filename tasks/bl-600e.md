Sighted closing bl-243b: the gate reported `src/tools/shell.rs: 46/47` and 99.95% overall, failing the 100% floor. The same tree had passed `scripts/check-coverage.sh` cleanly minutes earlier, and passed again on the re-run once the machine was quiet — so the line is reachable, and whether a run reaches it depends on load.

The suspect is `wait()` in src/tools/shell.rs: it polls `child.try_wait()` against a deadline, and the two arms — the child finished, and the deadline expired and the child was killed — are the two a test can only separate by timing. Under a loaded box one test's child crosses to the other side of its bound, and the arm that test exists to cover goes unexecuted. Nothing is wrong with the code; the TEST's bound is what is load-sensitive.

Why it matters beyond a re-run: a stored FAIL is what a verdict cache keeps, and the cost is paid by whoever closes next, on a tree they did not write. The fix belongs in the test, not the floor — make the deadline arm deterministic (a child that provably will not exit within any bound, so the kill is the only way the wait can end) rather than a race the scheduler adjudicates. Do NOT answer this by lowering the floor or excluding the file.

Reproduce by running the coverage gate with the box under real load; a quiet box will pass it every time, which is the whole defect.
