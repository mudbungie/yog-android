## The shapes

1. **`held` on the follow lane's tool-window entry** (yog bl-58bb). The
   capability control's reason for parking the call, beside the `tool_use`
   id and the tool name the entry already carries. A held invocation is
   parked before the executor is entered, so it lands neither `input.json`
   nor `output.json` and the lane reported the conversation at rest at the
   exact moment the operator was the thing it was waiting for. On the glass
   the chat's tool row wears the Attention state (STYLE.md §3: *asking for
   you* — a parked call held for an answer), which is the one place the six
   colours already had a word for this.
2. **`in_flight`, a fourth `framing` word on `reply/steps` rows** (yog
   bl-ab53). The step being written right now, told apart from the one an
   interrupt cut. The records screen paints `killed` today on every live
   step, which is the one word that makes a real interrupt legible.
3. **`/answer` gains a scope** (yog bl-94a5). The verdict controls stop being
   three bare words and gain a scope chooser, `once` the default.
4. **The proposals surface** (yog bl-dd88, `request/proposals`,
   `reply/proposals`, `request/proposal`). A new read and a new act, so
   `PROTOCOL` does not move for them — but a client gains them only by
   re-vendoring. The listing plus accept/reject belongs on the admin surface
   (§13.17), which is where the acts an operator takes on the world already
   live.
5. **`client` on the trail** (yog bl-e59e, PROTOCOL 16). Decoded and painted
   at bl-ee21; re-checked here so the 18 vendor states the whole set.
