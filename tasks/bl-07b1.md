yog bl-d1f1 stated the wire's lost-reply recovery contract in yog REMOTE §3
(new bullet ahead of "The disk inbox survives for in-world callers"): a
connection that dies between the engine completing an act and the reply frame
landing leaves the act IN DOUBT — no idempotency token rides the act
envelope, no redelivery slot exists for acts, and the recovery is a read of
the world, never a resend. Nothing wire-visible moved: PROTOCOL stands at 8,
the corpus is unchanged, nothing to re-vendor.

What the phone seat owes it — behavioral only:

1. Never auto-retry an act after a transport error (a dropped connection, a
   write that failed mid-frame, a read timeout after the act was written).
   Retrying a read is fine (yog REMOTE §9.7: asking twice is asking once);
   retrying an act is a second act (§9.8: two clicks of Nudge are two nudges).
2. Surface the failure as *in doubt* rather than *failed* where the act had
   been written before the transport died: the engine may have completed it,
   and the next read of the conversation/roster shows whether it did. This
   matters more on a phone than on the desktop — mobile radios drop
   connections routinely, so the tempting reconnect-and-replay loop is
   exactly the unsafe path the contract forbids.
