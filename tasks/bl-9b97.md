Operator ruling 2026-10-20: after bl-0db5 the phone still looks mostly like the old app. The earlier port (DESIGN §23, bl-d489) carried the mechanics of lernie §4.44–4.46 — tabs, fold stages, bubbles, help, knobs — and left the shape an operator actually sees: the floor's order, the rule's chips, the speaker lines and the step lines.

What differs, read off the emulator captures of 0.0.40 against the desktop 0.1.107:

1. Floor order. Desktop (lernie §4.45 item 3, bl-45ab): the rule takes the bottom edge, the dock stands on it, the box on those, the ask on the box — the words typed are beside the words answered. Phone (§22 item 1): the box is at the floor and the rule, the dock and the ask stand above it. §23 item 4 says the phone had the desktop's order first; it did not.
2. The rule. Desktop: one line of text runs, `— workspace ⏶ | provider ⏶ | model ⏶ | effort ⏶ | priority ⏶ | state · age | tokens | spend | context — name`, the mark inside each door, bare at rest and lit under the pointer, dropping from the right. Phone: a band of filled chip buttons with ▼ beside a large mark, the diagnostics on a second small line.
3. Speaker lines. Desktop: `user` and the model id, no colon, `<model id> (thinking)` on reasoning. Phone: `user:` and `<conversation name>:`, `thinking:` over its text.
4. Compact steps. Desktop: a finished call is its line with ✔/✖ and one weak line of output beneath; a finished thought or prose is `<who> · <first line>` elided in one. Phone: a label line over a text line.
5. The hint. Desktop: `say it — …`. Phone: `message`.

Children land each on main; the last walks the emulator and installs the signed APK on the phone over adb when it is unlocked.
