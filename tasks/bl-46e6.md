`scripts/screens.sh` sits 11 lines under the 300 wall (`make line-cap
LINE_CAP=199` for today's census). That is the state CLAUDE.md warns about: a
file resting on the wall fires on whoever touches it next, at the moment they
are finishing something else, when the cheapest way out is exactly the shave
the rule forbids.

Three seams have already been taken out of it and each is a different question:
`screens-seed.sh` (what puts the device on a screen), `screens-platform.sh`
(what the platform granted and bound), `screens-background.sh` (the beats that
MOVE the device), and `screens-reach.sh` (turning a rectangle the app reported
into a gesture, bl-f97c).

The seam left is the one the file's own header names twice: the harness's
INSTRUMENTS — `relaunch`, `probe`, `settle`, `verdict`, `capture` — against the
WALK, the numbered sequence of beats that spends them. The walk is what a
reader comes to this file for and what every new screen edits; the instruments
have not changed in several balls. Splitting them leaves `screens.sh` as the
beat list plus its preflight, which is what it should read as.

Not urgent and nothing is broken — the walk is green. Do it before the next
beat is added, not after.
