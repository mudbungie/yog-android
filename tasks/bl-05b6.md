DESIGN §16.1 / §6. Every tool this client advertises is host-tested and every one of them is advertised on the strength of that. What no test in this repo has ever done is put an invocation through the tool-host channel to a device and read the capture back — and the half that gap covers is exactly the half a host test cannot reach: a permission dialog answered, an activity launch the platform refuses for being in the background, a clip that really is on the clipboard, a battery figure that is this device's.

Rung 1 (bl-f34f) landed on the same discipline the nine before it did — host tests at the 100% floor, plus `make screens` proving the APK installs with its new permissions, boots, paints every screen and leaves parity green — and it went one step further where it was cheap: the four static methods the JNI resolves by name were read back out of the shipped dex with their descriptors, so `dev.yog.Paper.{device,clipboardSet,notify,open}` are known to exist at the signatures `tools/paper/bridge.rs` builds. That is the seam checked, not the platform.

What an on-device proof needs, and what does not exist yet:

- an engine with a wire listener, and a leaf of FOOT grade seeded into the app's own storage. `scripts/screens-seed.sh` already mints a seat leaf into exactly that place, so the seeding half is a known shape and the grade is the only change.
- the device registered in a workspace (REMOTE §1.5), which is what makes the advertisement visible to anything.
- something that fires `/invoke <client> <tool> <json>` at THAT engine and then reads the capture. **`yog gesture` will not do**: the invocation mailbox is per-process in-memory state, so a gesture run in a second process addresses a different mailbox than the one the phone's parked `invocations` read is waiting on. The seat has to dial the listener.

Attack the shape before building it. Two candidates and they answer different questions:

- a beat beside `scripts/screens.sh` — its own emulator, its own seed, one bounded run, four invocations, four captures compared. Proves the platform, costs an emulator, and cannot be part of `make check` for the same reason `screens` is not.
- a dex-descriptor assertion folded into `make apk` — pin every bridged class and method signature the crate resolves by name against the built dex, both directions. Cheap, mechanical, and catches a renamed Java method the day it is renamed rather than at the first invocation. It proves the seam and nothing about the platform, so it is a complement, not the answer.

The first is the ball. The second is small enough to ride along and is worth doing whether or not the first lands, because a JNI name is resolved at runtime and nothing in the tree checks it today.

Every later rung of the corpus inherits this gap — bl-b0a9 (camera, location), bl-5cbd (the notification listener), bl-8bd0 (the pocketed foot) — and each of them is HARDER to argue from host tests than rung 1 was, because more of what they do is the platform.
