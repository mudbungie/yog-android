The enrollment screen wants **Scan QR** beside the paste field (bl-dd7b). Decoding a QR from the camera needs a decoder, and the honest evaluation is that **the platform ships none this app can reach** — so this is rule 6 territory: *"Zero new dependencies without explicit operator approval."*

**What was looked for and what is there.**

- **AOSP framework**: no barcode or QR decoder in any public API. `camera2`/CameraX give frames; nothing in the platform reads one. There is no `ACTION_SCAN_BARCODE` a stranger app may rely on, and routing through the system camera app or Lens is a hand-off to whatever happens to be installed, with no contract and no result on devices without it.
- **ML Kit barcode scanning** (`com.google.mlkit:barcode-scanning`, or `play-services-mlkit-barcode-scanning` + the Google Code Scanner): the route everyone means by "the platform route". It is **not** platform: it is Google Play Services, a Gradle dependency tree this repo has never had, several MB in the APK for the bundled model, and absent on a device without Play Services. It also brings CameraX or a camera2 pipeline with it.
- **ZXing**: third-party, either as `com.google.zxing:core` (Java, into the Gradle module) or `rxing` (a Rust port, into `Cargo.toml` — which is where this crate's gate can actually judge a dependency: `cargo-deny` reads licenses, advisories, sources and bans, and the Gradle side has no equivalent gate at all).
- **From scratch**: a QR decoder is a Reed-Solomon decode behind a binarizer, a finder-pattern locate and a perspective unwarp. Buildable, testable, and not a small evening — and it would be new code under the 100% floor for a format that has three mature implementations.

**The decision is the operator's, and it is a real one**, because the three routes differ in what they cost the shape of the app rather than in convenience: ML Kit makes the APK depend on Play Services, `rxing` makes it a Rust dependency the existing gate can judge, and a hand-rolled decoder makes it code this repo maintains forever.

**Nothing is blocked on it.** bl-dd7b lands the paste path, which is the QR's degraded path and has to exist regardless; a camera route is a decoder feeding that same already-proven sink, and it is one screen and one permission on top.

Also needed either way, and not free: `CAMERA` in the manifest, a runtime permission request, a preview surface, and the refusal to paint when permission is denied — none of which the app has today.
